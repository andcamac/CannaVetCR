# Cannabis/Hemp in Livestock & Food-Producing Animals

**This is the one area where the answer for a clinic-facing agent should be short and firm: it is not currently legal, and the agent should say so rather than offer a dosing framework.**

## Legal status (US)

- Hemp and hemp byproducts (CBD oil, spent hemp biomass, hemp seed cake/meal, etc.) remain **ineligible for commercial use in US animal agriculture** — any use in feed requires FDA-CVM approval, which has not been granted.
- AAFCO (Association of American Feed Control Officials) has repeatedly stated that no cannabinoid-containing hemp ingredient is currently listed in its Official Publication as an approved feed ingredient, and continues to ask industry for more safety data before considering approval.
- The core regulatory concern is **residue carryover**: cannabinoids (primarily THC and CBD) can be considered contaminants in hemp seed byproducts if plant material isn't fully removed during processing, creating a risk that they end up in meat, milk, or eggs consumed by humans.
- A pending application to approve hempseed cake for laying hens has been under FDA/AAFCO review for years, slowed by exactly this residue-carryover concern.
- One narrow exception: **hempseed meal for poultry** has moved toward a tentative AAFCO definition on the basis that it contains virtually no detectable cannabinoids — but state-by-state adoption is still uneven, and this covers only that specific ingredient, not hemp biomass, oil, or CBD generally.

## Dairy cattle residue data

- A 2025 study (*J Agric Food Chem*) specifically modeled cannabinoid distribution and clearance when spent hemp biomass was fed to dairy cows, examining potential Δ9-THC exposure to humans via milk consumption — this is the kind of residue study regulators are waiting on before any broader approval.

## State patchwork

- A small number of states (e.g., Montana) permit some hemp feeding to certain animals, but with the explicit condition that those animals (and their products) **cannot enter interstate or even intrastate commerce**, since they'd be considered "adulterated" under federal law.
- California's Department of Food and Agriculture has issued explicit notices that no hemp or cannabis ingredient — feed or livestock drug — is approved for livestock use in the state, deferring entirely to the federal AAFCO/FDA approval process.

## What this means for an agent

- If a user asks about CBD/hemp dosing for cattle, swine, poultry, sheep, goats, or any other food-producing species: the agent should **not** return a dosing range. It should state that no ingredient is currently FDA/AAFCO-approved for this use, flag the human food-residue concern as the reason, and suggest the vet/owner contact their state department of agriculture or feed control office for current status.
- This applies even where an owner describes the animal as a "pet" (e.g., a backyard hen or a horse) if its products (eggs, milk) could enter the food supply — flag ambiguity rather than assume it's a pure companion animal.
- Companion horses not in food production are covered under `04-equine.md` instead — the "no feed approval" restriction is about the food-supply chain, not horses as a species per se.
