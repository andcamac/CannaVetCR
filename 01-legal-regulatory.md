# Legal & Regulatory Landscape

## Federal status

- The **2018 Farm Bill** removed hemp (cannabis with ≤0.3% THC by dry weight) from the Controlled Substances Act definition of marijuana, descheduling hemp itself — but it did **not** create an approval pathway for hemp-derived ingredients in animal food or drugs. Authority to schedule hemp-derived products intended for therapeutic use was explicitly left intact. [AVMA, "Cannabis in Veterinary Medicine" report, 2026]
- **FDA-CVM (Center for Veterinary Medicine)** takes the position that CBD/hemp products marketed with therapeutic claims for animals are **unapproved new animal drugs**. No cannabinoid product currently has FDA approval for veterinary use.
- In 2025 the FDA opened a public request-for-information docket specifically on cannabis-derived products in veterinary medicine, signaling review of submitted safety data and the possibility of formal compliance standards or further enforcement action in 2026. [CannabisRegulations.ai, "Pet CBD Under Review," 2025]
- The FDA has issued warning letters to pet-CBD companies over disease-treatment marketing claims: in April 2025 four manufacturers (CBD Dog Health, MycoDog, Bailey's CBD, HolistaPet) received letters citing illegal claims, including cancer-treatment claims. [Tails' Talks, citing Vicente LLP analysis of FDA warning letters, 2026]

## AVMA / veterinary association position

- The American Association of Veterinary State Boards (AAVSB) has taken the position that veterinarians should be able to *discuss* CBD with clients as a matter of animal and public protection — since clients can obtain these products easily elsewhere and are actively seeking guidance. [AVMA/JAVMA News, "The hype and hope of veterinary cannabis," 2020]
- Most states have no law explicitly banning hemp-CBD use in animals, but few states shield veterinarians from board discipline for recommending it — which leaves many practitioners cautious about doing so even where it's not technically illegal. The AVMA's stated position supports discussing the full range of treatment options, including off-label approaches. [Today's Veterinary Practice, 2024]
- The AVMA published a comprehensive 2025–2026 practitioner guide, "Cannabis in Veterinary Medicine," summarizing the science, terminology, and regulatory status, explicitly framed around the range of cannabis-related situations vets now face — from a dog that ingested an owner's marijuana to client questions about legality and efficacy of pet cannabis products. [dvm360, "AVMA releases CBD resource guide," 2026]

## State-level variation (illustrative, not exhaustive — verify current status before relying on it)

- **Maryland**: new law (effective October 1, 2026) protects veterinarians from professional discipline solely for discussing or recommending cannabis/CBD products for animals. This is a speech/professional-judgment protection statute, not full legalization of veterinary cannabis use. [WeedPress, "Maryland Just Drew a New Line on Veterinary Cannabis," April 2026]
- State veterinary medical associations differ sharply: Michigan warns of added legal risk if an adverse event occurs; Connecticut has reportedly been told by its state consumer affairs department that CBD is illegal for animal use; Oregon aligns with the FDA position that hemp-CBD products are unapproved animal drugs; Illinois circulates AVMA guidance against disease-treatment claims; Oklahoma flags liability risk despite legality; several other states (including Colorado, New Jersey, Washington, Florida, Texas, and New York) have issued no formal position at all. [dvm360, "The future of alternative medicine: let's talk about cannabidiol"]
- AAVSB has published model guidance documents meant to give licensing boards and clients a consistent basis for CBD use within a defined treatment plan. [AAVSB, "Guidance Document for CBD Use on Companion Animals," 2026]

## Practical implications for an agent/tool

1. **Never present a dose as a prescription.** Present it as "published study X used Y mg/kg for Z condition, in N animals, over duration D."
2. **Flag state variability.** Don't assume dosing/recommendation guidance is uniformly legal to relay in every jurisdiction; prompt the user to confirm their state board's current position before acting on it clinically.
3. **Distinguish CBD/hemp (federally descheduled, state-variable regulation) from THC/marijuana (still Schedule I federally, separately state-variable)** — legal treatment differs substantially between the two.
4. **Food animals are categorically different** — see `05-livestock-food-animals.md`. It is not currently legal to use cannabis/hemp derivatives in feed or as a drug in food-producing species in the US, full stop.
