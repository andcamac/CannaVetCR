# Legal & Regulatory Landscape — Costa Rica (primary jurisdiction)

This clinic operates in Costa Rica, where the FDA has no jurisdiction. Costa Rica has its own cannabis framework, summarized here. US federal information is kept afterward as background context only, because most of the *clinical/dosing literature* referenced throughout this knowledge base originates in the US, EU, and other countries — that's a separate question from which regulator has legal authority over a Costa Rican clinic.

## The governing law: Ley N.º 10113 (2022)

Costa Rica's cannabis framework is **Ley N.º 10113**, *"Ley del Cannabis para Uso Medicinal y Terapéutico y del Cáñamo para Uso Alimentario e Industrial,"* signed March 2, 2022. It creates two tracks:

1. **Psychoactive medicinal/therapeutic cannabis** for human patients — any licensed physician (Colegio de Médicos y Cirujanos) can prescribe it, not just specialists.
2. **Industrial/food-use hemp (cáñamo)** — defined under Costa Rican law as cannabis with **less than 1% THC**. This is a materially different threshold than the US's 0.3% hemp standard, which matters if a clinic is evaluating a product imported from the US. [Ley N.º 10113, full text (PGR SCIJ)](https://pgrweb.go.cr/scij/Busqueda/Normativa/Normas/nrm_texto_completo.aspx?param1=NRTC&nValor1=1&nValor2=96518&nValor3=130412&strTipM=TC)

## Implementation timeline and who regulates what

The law passed in 2022 but sat largely dormant as a paper framework for nearly three years. The implementing regulation (*Reglamento*) was approved in **February 2025**, the product technical standard **RTCR 515:2024** has been in force since **June 2025**, and a seed-to-sale traceability mandate is phasing in with full compliance due around **February 2027**.

Three authorities share oversight:
- **MAG** (Ministerio de Agricultura y Ganadería) — cultivation/production licensing, seed approval
- **Ministerio de Salud** — medical product registration
- **ICD** (Instituto Costarricense sobre Drogas) — control and traceability

[Reglamento del Cannabis para Uso Medicinal y Terapéutico](https://vlex.co.cr/vid/reglamento-cannabis-uso-medicinal-1094373332)

## Where veterinary use fits — and where it doesn't yet

Ley 10113 is written around **human** medicinal use. It does **not** explicitly authorize veterinarians to prescribe psychoactive cannabis for animal patients the way it authorizes physicians for human patients. For comparison: Argentina's cannabis regulation was explicitly amended in 2023 to add veterinary use as one of six authorized categories — Costa Rica's law has no equivalent express provision as of this research (September 2026).

Separately, though, **SENASA's Dirección de Medicamentos Veterinarios** (under MAG) runs a general veterinary product registration pathway, and at least one **THC-free, hemp-derived CBD product for dogs and cats is already commercially registered through it** — for example, a pet CBD oil carrying SENASA/MAG registration number `LIC DAA-MAG 155-659`.

**In practice:** THC-free hemp/CBD pet products can reach the Costa Rican market through the standard veterinary product registry, while psychoactive (THC-containing) cannabis use in animals sits in a genuine legal gray area that Ley 10113 does not explicitly address.

[SENASA — Dirección de Medicamentos Veterinarios](http://www.senasa.go.cr/133-cat-institucion/cat-organizacion/cat-direcciones-nacionales/direccion-de-medicamentos-veterinarios)

## Recreational status and enforcement

Recreational marijuana use and possession **remain illegal** in Costa Rica; only the medicinal/therapeutic and industrial/food hemp tracks under Ley 10113 are lawful. Police commonly do not pursue simple possession of small amounts in practice, but this is enforcement discretion, not legal authorization — it is not a basis for a clinical recommendation.

## Practical implications for the clinic

1. **THC-free CBD/hemp products**: confirm the specific product carries a valid **SENASA/MAG veterinary registration** before recommending or selling it. A product registered with the FDA in the US is *not* automatically registered in Costa Rica.
2. **Anything containing THC**: there is no explicit legal pathway for veterinary prescribing under current law. Treat it as an open legal question, not a recommendation to make.
3. **Client-sourced human medicinal cannabis on a pet**: if a client wants to use their own physician-prescribed product (obtained under Ley 10113) on their pet, that use falls entirely outside the animal-health regulatory framework — flag both the legal gap and the toxicity risk (see `06-toxicity-emergency.md`).
4. **Confirm before relying on this**: implementation is still rolling out through 2027. Check current status directly with SENASA or the **Colegio de Profesionales en Medicina Veterinaria de Costa Rica (CPMVCR)** before relying on anything here for a specific case. No public CPMVCR position statement on cannabis/CBD specifically was found during this research (September 2026) — that is an absence of a public statement, not confirmation that none exists; it's worth asking the Colegio directly. [CPMVCR](https://colegioveterinarios.or.cr/)

## For reference: US federal framework

Included because most cited dosing/safety literature is US/international in origin, and because the FDA's stance explains the "preclinical only, not a treatment claim" framing used throughout `08-oncology.md` — not because the FDA has any authority in Costa Rica.

- The **2018 Farm Bill** descheduled US hemp (≤0.3% THC) but created no approval pathway for hemp-derived ingredients in animal food or drugs.
- **FDA-CVM** treats CBD/hemp products marketed with therapeutic claims as unapproved new animal drugs. No cannabinoid product currently has FDA approval for veterinary use.
- In April 2025 the FDA sent warning letters to four pet-CBD manufacturers over illegal disease-treatment marketing claims, **including cancer claims** — directly relevant context for how the Cancer/Oncology section of this knowledge base is worded.
- Most US state veterinary boards have no explicit ban on hemp-CBD discussion, but few explicitly protect veterinarians from board discipline for recommending it, so practice varies state to state. This has no direct bearing on a Costa Rican clinic but explains why US-sourced client materials/product marketing are worded cautiously.

## Practical implications for an agent/tool

1. **Lead with Costa Rican law**, not US federal status, for any legal question from this clinic.
2. **Never present a dose as a prescription** — present it as "published study X used Y mg/kg for Z condition."
3. **Distinguish CBD/hemp (< 1% THC under CR law) from THC/psychoactive cannabis** — the two tracks under Ley 10113 have very different legal treatment, and animal use is only clearly addressed for the former (via SENASA product registration), not the latter.
4. **Food animals remain a hard no** regardless of jurisdiction — see `05-livestock-food-animals.md`.
