# Cannabinoids in Dogs

Dogs are by far the most-studied veterinary species for cannabinoids — most published pharmacokinetic and clinical-trial data is canine.

## Osteoarthritis / chronic pain

- **Foundational RCT**: Gamble et al. (2018), *Frontiers in Veterinary Science* — osteoarthritic dogs given CBD showed improved comfort/activity scores versus placebo over a 4-week trial, with no significant adverse effects reported at the doses tested (2 mg/kg twice daily was a commonly cited effective arm in follow-on summaries). This is the most-cited canine CBD efficacy study.
- Doses used in the osteoarthritis/pain literature commonly cluster around **2 mg/kg CBD, twice daily**, sometimes escalated to 4–8 mg/kg/day in tolerance studies.
- A related study examined oral transmucosal CBD oil as part of a **multimodal analgesic regimen** (i.e., adjunct to, not replacement for, NSAIDs/other analgesics) for canine OA pain.

## Epilepsy

- McGrath et al. (2019), *JAVMA* (Colorado State University) — randomized, blinded, placebo-controlled trial of CBD **added to existing anti-epileptic drug therapy** in dogs with intractable idiopathic epilepsy; found a reduction in seizure frequency in the CBD group.
- Rozental et al. (2023), *J Vet Intern Med* — double-blinded crossover study in 51 dogs with drug-resistant idiopathic epilepsy, evaluating CBD as adjunct treatment; part of a growing (but still small) evidence base specifically for epilepsy.
- **Framing for an agent**: epilepsy CBD use in the literature is consistently *adjunctive* to standard anti-epileptic drugs, not a monotherapy replacement.

## Atopic dermatitis / itching

- A single positive trial (the "CannPal" canine atopic dermatitis study, summarized by Cornell) found roughly 65% of treated dogs had at least a 50% reduction in itching/scratching behavior, with about half showing full resolution — but this is **one trial, not yet replicated**, so evidence strength here is lower than for OA or epilepsy.

## Safety / tolerability / pharmacokinetics

- Bartner et al. (2018), *Canadian Journal of Veterinary Research* — pharmacokinetics of CBD administered by three delivery methods (oil, capsule, transdermal cream) at two dosages in healthy dogs.
- Deabold et al. (2019), *Animals* — single-dose pharmacokinetics and preliminary safety of CBD-rich hemp nutraceutical in healthy dogs and cats.
- Bradley/Bakke et al. (2022), *Frontiers in Veterinary Science* — long-term (6-month) daily feeding of CBD (~4 mg/kg) was well tolerated in healthy dogs.
- A 2020 escalating-dose safety study in Beagles found dogs tolerated dose escalation of CBD oil well, with no major adverse findings reported.
- A separate "Report of Adverse Effects" study (30 healthy Beagles, 10–20 mg/kg/day across capsule/oil/transdermal forms) also concluded CBD was well tolerated, though elevated liver enzymes (ALP) have been a recurring, mild lab finding across several canine studies and warrant monitoring on longer-term use.

## Practical dosing notes appearing across consumer/veterinary-adjacent sources (lower evidence tier — not RCT-sourced)

- General starting-dose heuristics circulating in pet-CBD guidance: roughly **0.1–0.5 mg/kg**, split into two daily doses, with re-evaluation after 5–7 days before increasing.
- These lower-tier sources are useful for *typical commercial product* framing but should be clearly flagged in the agent as lower evidence quality than the peer-reviewed RCTs above.

## Cautions specific to dogs

- Elevated liver enzymes (particularly ALP) have shown up as a recurring, usually mild, lab finding in multiple long-term dosing studies — baseline and periodic bloodwork is a reasonable safeguard for any planned multi-week course.
- Dogs have a much higher density of cannabinoid receptors than humans, which is part of why THC-containing (as opposed to CBD/hemp) products are considerably more likely to cause toxicosis in dogs at doses that wouldn't affect a person — see `06-toxicity-emergency.md`.
