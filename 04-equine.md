# Cannabinoids in Horses

## Osteoarthritis / lameness / pain

- Interlandi et al. (2024), *Frontiers in Veterinary Science* — oral transmucosal CBD oil as part of a multimodal analgesic regimen improved quality-of-life and pain measures in mature horses with osteoarthritis, in a 20-horse study alongside a conventional analgesic protocol.
- A separate 2024 study (24 horses, 11–18 years old, chronic OA) compared **CBDA 15% oil** against a **CBG/CBD blend (20%/10%)** over 14 consecutive days for pain alleviation — one of the few head-to-head cannabinoid-formulation comparisons in any veterinary species.
- Earlier case-level work also reported CBD-oil use for **mechanical allodynia** in a single horse, and for second-intention wound healing and stereotypic behaviors (e.g., crib-biting) — these are lower-evidence, case-report-level findings rather than controlled trials.

## Pharmacokinetics

- Ryan et al. (2021), *Drug Testing and Analysis* — single oral doses of 0.5, 1, and 2 mg/kg CBD in exercising Thoroughbreds (3-way crossover), examining pharmacokinetics, behavioral/physiologic effects, and inflammatory biomarkers.
- Yocom/Contino et al. — pharmacokinetics, safety, and **synovial fluid concentrations** of single- and multiple-dose 1 and 3 mg/kg CBD in horses; notably found no clear evidence that CBD reaches detectable levels in synovial fluid despite CBD products being marketed for joint/lameness relief — a meaningful caveat for efficacy claims.
- A 2026 pharmacokinetic study in Connemara ponies examined CBD metabolic effects over 21 days of treatment.
- A 2025 chronic-dosing safety study (*American Journal of Veterinary Research*) found full-spectrum CBD/CBDA hemp oil extracts had no adverse effects over a multi-week dosing period, and that **CBDA reaches substantially higher serum concentrations than CBD** at equivalent doses (in that study, roughly 8x higher peak concentration for CBDA vs. CBD).
- A single-dose pharmacokinetics/tolerability study of CBD and CBDA-rich hemp compared 2 mg/kg vs. 8 mg/kg doses, tracking manure production and GI transit time for 48 hours post-administration as a tolerability proxy.

## Competition / drug-testing considerations (unique to equine)

- Most international equine sport governing bodies prohibit cannabinoids as competition substances.
- In 2022, the **Fédération Équestre Internationale (FEI)** reclassified CBD and CBDA from "prohibited" to "controlled medication" — meaning detection can still trigger an adverse analytical finding, but it's evaluated under different rules than banned substances. This is a critical practical flag for any competition or performance-horse client.

## Practical implications for an agent

- Horse-specific doses in the literature cluster in the **0.5–3 mg/kg** range for pharmacokinetic/pain studies, generally lower per kg than typical canine dosing, though horses' much larger absolute body mass means total mg amounts are far higher.
- Flag the synovial-fluid-penetration caveat prominently: marketed joint-relief claims for equine CBD outpace what's been demonstrated pharmacokinetically.
- Always ask about competition status before recommending any cannabinoid product for a performance horse, given FEI's controlled-medication classification.
